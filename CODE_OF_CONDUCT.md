# Code of conduct

Lightify is a small project, and we'd like it to stay a friendly place to report bugs,
suggest ideas and contribute code. Everyone who takes part in issues, pull requests,
discussions or any other space for this project is expected to follow this code.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new or who write in a language
  that isn't their first.
- Assume good intent, and ask before assuming the worst.
- Keep criticism about the work, not the person, and make it useful.
- Accept a "no" on an idea or a pull request gracefully; it isn't a judgement of you.
- Respect people's time: search before filing, and give the details asked for.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or deliberate intimidation, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory jokes or language, or attacks on anyone's identity, including their age,
  body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience, nationality,
  appearance, race, religion or sexual orientation.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address, email or account
  details, without their permission.
- Spam, trolling, and repeatedly derailing a conversation.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour here worries you, report it privately to the maintainer. Use this
repository's private report form (the **Security** tab, then **Report a vulnerability**)
and say that it's a conduct report; only the maintainer can see it. You can also use
GitHub's own **Report content** and block features.

Reports are handled in confidence, and the person who reported is kept out of it unless
they agree otherwise.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides how to respond to behaviour that breaks this code. Depending on
how serious it is, that can be a private word, a public note, removing comments or
commits, or a temporary or permanent ban from the project.

## Scope and credit

This code applies in every project space, and when someone represents the project in
public. It's written in the spirit of the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org).
